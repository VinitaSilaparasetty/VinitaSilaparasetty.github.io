Case study · Curriculum design, 2025

From Participant Manual to Accreditation-Ready Course in Five Weeks

The problem: FormativeLabz, a Canadian training provider, had a course concept and a participant manual. It had no teaching materials, a launch only weeks away, and a CPD accreditation review still to pass.

The result: I designed and built two complete, assessed modules (6 skills) and uploaded them to The CPD Group’s accreditation portal myself. Nine days later, the course was accredited for 23 CPD hours, and it then went live for learners on Moodle.

“The CPD Group needed complete course materials and assessments before it would accredit the course. I built them, uploaded them to the accreditation portal myself, and nine days later the course was accredited for 23 CPD hours.”

The challenge

FormativeLabz faced the same problems most training providers hit when they launch a new certification.

How I work

  1. Discover. I met the FormativeLabz team, put my profile forward for the CPD submission, and studied the participant manual’s learning outcomes for all four modules.
  2. Scope. I agreed on two modules (2 and 3) so I could go deep rather than wide.
  3. Propose. I sent a structured teaching plan covering interactive live sessions, free industry tools and a formative-plus-summative assessment strategy. I then added a capstone project per module and suggested that all instructors agree on shared tools.
  4. Design and build. I created the decks, walkthroughs, worksheets and capstone scenario for Skills 4–9 (see What I built, below).
  5. Hand over. On 17 February 2025 I uploaded the slides, worksheets and assessments to The CPD Group’s accreditation portal. I then loaded everything into FormativeLabz’s Moodle LMS. Moodle would not accept the Excel datasets, so they were moved to Google Drive. All materials were live for learners by 28 March 2025.

What I built

My two modules form the practical core of the certification. Module 1 lays the foundations of AI and PIPEDA, my modules teach learners to collect, analyze, act on and measure customer data, and Module 4 looks ahead.

I structured them as one continuous data life cycle. Each skill’s output feeds the next: compliant data → segments → tested strategy → personalized content → engagement → measured ROI.

Learning outcomes covered. These skills deliver the manual’s outcome II (Utilize data-driven marketing strategies) and outcome III (Enhance customer engagement with AI). Every skill keeps Canadian privacy compliance in view.

A consistent session pattern. Every skill runs the same sequence:

  1. Learning objectives and a “by the end of this session, you will be able to…” statement
  2. Core concept
  3. Why marketers care
  4. Real-company case study
  5. Tool introduction
  6. Step-by-step workflow
  7. Hands-on activity
  8. Recap

The repeated pattern lowers the learning curve for a new topic and makes every session easy to map to CPD learning outcomes.

Assessment strategy

I used three layers of assessment: frequent formative checks, a summative assessment at the end of each module, and a capstone project that ties every skill to one realistic business.

LayerWhenFormPurpose
FormativeLive in every session and midway through each moduleMentimeter polls and quizzes, discussion questions, worksheet activitiesShow me and the learner the knowledge gaps while there is still time to close them
SummativeEnd of each moduleAssessment against the module’s learning outcomesEvidence of achievement for certification and CPD
CapstoneAcross each moduleApplied project on the Maple Trends scenarioHands-on project experience, as the brief required

The Maple Trends capstone. Maple Trends is a fictional Canadian e-commerce and retail fashion brand. It wants to launch a personalized email campaign while staying PIPEDA-compliant. Learners carry the brand through every skill:

  • Skill 4: define the purpose, the data types and sources, the consent method, role-based access, an 18-month retention rule and a breach-response plan.
  • Skill 5: segment customers by behaviour and value in Tableau.
  • Skill 6: design a GA4 / Google Ads A/B test.
  • Skill 7: write personalized email and ad variants with ChatGPT.
  • Skill 8: build a Flowise FAQ chatbot.
  • Skill 9: set the KPIs, a baseline and an ROI measurement plan.

A single fictional company makes the module feel like one real project, not six separate exercises. It also avoids using any real customer data, which suits a course about privacy.

What worked

  • Agreeing scope early. Settling on two modules instead of three left time for depth: worked examples, walkthroughs and a capstone for every skill.
  • Building to the standard from day one. Writing to CPD criteria from the start, rather than retrofitting them, meant the materials were ready for review as soon as they were uploaded.
  • Free tools and one running scenario. No learner needs paid software, and the Maple Trends capstone carries one story from data collection to ROI.
